# Writing the Discussion

Use only the moves relevant to the requested argument and genre; the weather/climate details below are conditional. These are diagnostics, not mandatory headings or sentence templates.

## Subsection structure and balance

Divide every Discussion into distinct, meaningfully titled subsections. Use strong, informative names that identify each subsection's scientific purpose or supported finding. Choose the number and order to fit the argument. The moves below guide the content and do not require one subsection per move.

Explain the supported strengths, contributions and scientific or practical value alongside honest criticism of limitations, adverse findings and unresolved questions. Recognise benefits only where the paper established them. Relate criticism to the interpretations or uses it constrained, using diplomatic and constructive wording while preserving material weaknesses and uncertainty.

## Move 1: Synthesize the answer

Restate the scientific answer without repeating the Results section numerically.

## Move 2: Synthesize mechanisms

Connect the local interpretations already established in Results to governing physical relationships. Compare supported mechanisms with plausible alternatives; do not postpone all interpretation until this section.

## Move 3: Situate the result

Explain how the finding changes current weather/climate modeling, forecasting, or understanding and where it agrees or conflicts with prior work.

## Move 4: Partition uncertainty

Discuss sampling, observations, initial conditions, internal variability, model structure, forcing/scenario, and methodological uncertainty as applicable.

## Move 5: State limitations by consequence

Concentrate general limitations and scope qualifications here or in a dedicated Scope section, using neutral language. Identify which conclusions, domains, regimes, resolutions, lead times or uses are limited and distinguish missing evidence from known failure. Retain validity-critical conditions beside the results they constrain, while consolidating repeated caveats rather than weakening each supported finding.

## Move 6: Define the next decisive test

Propose the experiment or observation that would resolve the most important remaining uncertainty. Avoid generic future-work lists.

## Move 7: End on the consequential implication

Make the final sentence of a standalone Conclusion, or of the Discussion when it serves as the conclusion, concise, specific, and impactful. State the strongest scientific understanding, forecasting capability, modeling consequence, or decision-relevant implication established by the evidence. Preserve the paper's scope and uncertainty, but do not end with administrative detail, a citation, a generic call for more research, a repeated limitation, or a claim introduced only for rhetorical force.

Write a standalone Conclusion and any concluding Discussion passage in past tense. Follow [the tense requirements](../references/prose_style.md#conclusions-and-descriptions-of-papers) when recounting the contribution, findings, implications and unresolved questions.

Read the final sentence independently. It should answer why the established result matters and leave the reader with the paper's durable contribution, not merely restate that the study was conducted.

Results and Discussion may be combined when the genre or venue benefits from immediate process interpretation. Divide the combined section into meaningful titled subsections as well, preserving the distinction between observation, local interpretation, synthesis and limitation.
