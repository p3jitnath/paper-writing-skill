# Writing the Introduction

Use only the moves relevant to the requested argument and genre; the weather/climate details below are conditional. These are diagnostics, not mandatory headings or sentence templates.

## Opening and citation requirements

When drafting or substantially revising a complete introduction, reserve the first two prose paragraphs exclusively for setting the scene:

1. Establish the broader scientific background, phenomenon, or problem and why it matters.
2. Narrow to the relevant setting, scales, and scientific or practical motivation, defining the context needed to understand the later argument.

Relevant background citations belong in these paragraphs. Begin the explicit research gap, proposed approach or model, novelty, contributions, and results preview from the third paragraph onward. Do not compress the setup into one paragraph or introduce the paper's own method or findings during the opening two paragraphs.

Use a minimum of five and a maximum of ten distinct cited sources across the entire introduction. Count unique works, not citation commands: each source in a grouped citation counts separately, repeated citations to the same work count once, and duplicate bibliography records for one publication do not increase the count. The limit applies to the whole introduction, not to each paragraph.

Use verified, relevant sources and place each citation beside the claim it supports. Do not pad the count with irrelevant references, invent sources, or remove necessary support merely to meet the maximum. Select the most pertinent sources and consolidate overlapping claims where appropriate. If five verified sources are unavailable, identify the missing support and complete independent drafting without presenting the citation requirement as satisfied.

Before delivery, inspect the first two paragraphs for their setup role and mechanically count the distinct sources cited in the intended accepted introduction, excluding comments, deleted review text, and uncited bibliography entries. A local wording correction does not authorise restructuring unrelated paragraphs; flag any relevant existing shortfall instead.

Write Introduction 0 before finalizing experiments, then rewrite the final introduction after Results and Discussion stabilize.

## Move 1: Scientific stakes

Use the first two paragraphs to establish the scientific setting and stakes as described above. For weather or climate work, name the Earth-system phenomenon, scale, and consequence. Prefer a concrete forecast, modeling, or understanding problem over generic climate urgency.

## Move 2: Knowledge or capability gap

Explain what current physical, statistical, or ML approaches cannot establish and why. Distinguish missing accuracy, missing physical credibility, missing resolution, excessive cost, and missing understanding.

## Move 3: Core insight

State the physical or methodological insight that makes progress possible. Do not lead with the model name.

## Move 4: Approach

Explain how the data, model, or diagnostic operationalizes the insight. Include the task, scale, and evidence boundary.

## Move 5: Contributions as findings

State contributions as claims with evidence. Separate predictive, physical, computational, and operational contributions instead of allowing one metric to imply all four.

## Move 6: Results preview and limits

Give the main result with metric, reference, domain, period, and uncertainty needed for interpretation. State the most consequential scope limit when omission would mislead.

## Venue adaptation

Earth-science journals usually foreground the scientific question and physical interpretation. ML conferences require a sharper method distinction and controlled experimental contribution. Preserve the same evidence boundary in both tracks.
